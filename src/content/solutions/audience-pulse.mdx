## The problem

Owners are heads-down in delivery and miss the slow shifts that matter: a new objection
creeping into sales calls, a competitor's message everyone starts echoing, an economic
pressure changing what the buyer will spend on. By the time it's obvious, the messaging is
already stale. Generic "industry news" digests don't help — they're not about *this*
business's audience and offers.

## What it does

Audience Pulse runs on a schedule (weekly or monthly) and monitors the owner's specific
audience for meaningful change:

- Emerging concerns and frustrations
- Questions being asked more often
- Industry, economic, technology, and regulatory developments that touch this audience
- Market trends and buying-behavior changes
- Terminology shifts and language the audience is tired of
- New objections and new opportunities

For each finding it separates **what is happening** from **why it matters**, and translates
it into messaging implications, language to watch, content opportunities, and offer
implications. The guiding distinction: Audience Pulse answers *"what should I know?"*, not
*"what should I write?"*

## Who it is for

Intelligence-tier owners — typically a bit more established, marketing actively, and willing
to pay for a standing advantage rather than one-off tools.

## How it works

1. An Intelligence-tier capability inside **Business Growth Advisor**. Requires the upgrade.
2. Uses the shared profile (audience, segments, problems, offers, positioning) to keep
   research narrow and specific to the business.
3. A modular research layer gathers current external signals with source tracking, then the
   model interprets them against the stored context.
4. Findings are stored with timestamps and sources. Each cycle compares against the last so
   the report can flag *new since last time* vs *still true* vs *fading*.
5. Findings feed other tools: audience language → Message Market-Fit Checker; concerns and
   objections → What Should I Promote? and the Intelligence Brief.

## Why it is worth building

- It is the core of the Intelligence tier's value: the product keeps working when the owner
  isn't logged in.
- It compounds. A stored history of what changed becomes something a competitor's generic
  tool can't replicate.
- It de-risks the rest of the suite — every other tool gets sharper when it knows what's
  currently moving in the market.

## Build phases

### Phase 1 — Manual-trigger single report
Owner clicks "run pulse". Research layer pulls current signals for the stored audience;
model returns findings split into happening / why-it-matters / implications. Store with
sources.

### Phase 2 — Scheduled and comparative
Weekly/monthly schedule. Historical storage and a diff against the prior cycle
("new / still important / fading / no longer significant"). Duplicate suppression.

### Phase 3 — Wired into the suite
Push findings to Message Market-Fit Checker, What Should I Promote?, and the Business Growth
Intelligence Brief. Add competitor-signal monitoring and emerging-buyer-language extraction
as sub-modules. Off-cycle alerts for high-threshold events.

## Stack and dependencies

- SOTS Connect shell, auth, **Intelligence entitlement gate**, shared profile
- A reusable research/validation layer with source + freshness tracking (shared with the Intelligence Brief)
- Anthropic API for interpretation; strict separation of sourced fact vs AI inference
- `audience_pulse_reports` + `research_findings` tables; `previously_reported_findings` for de-dup
- Scheduler / job runner (shared with Scheduled Report Jobs)

## Open questions and risks

- **Research quality and cost.** External research per cycle per user has a real API/token
  cost; needs source allow-listing and caching.
- **Prompt-injection.** External pages are untrusted input — must be sandboxed from
  instructions.
- **"So what" gap.** The hard part is not gathering signals, it's the *why it matters for
  you* translation. That's where the value or the disappointment lives.
- **Cadence.** Weekly may be too noisy for slow markets; monthly too slow for fast ones.
  Per-user configurable.
- **Overlap with the Intelligence Brief.** Pulse is the research engine; the Brief is the
  packaged deliverable. Keep the boundary clear or they cannibalize each other.
