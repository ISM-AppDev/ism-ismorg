## The problem

Happy customers rarely write testimonials. A blank box and "tell us about your experience"
is too much work, so you chase people for weeks and get a trickle of thin one-liners. The
genuinely good social proof stays stuck in people's heads.

## What it does

Sends the customer a short, tap-to-answer set of questions — which product or service,
overall experience, the biggest benefit, the service experience, how likely they are to
recommend it, and what they'd tell someone considering it. No typing. From those choices it
drafts **four or five distinct testimonials** in the customer's voice, varied in length and
angle. They pick one, tweak it if they want, or regenerate for more options — then confirm
their name, title, and permission to use it.

## Who it is for

Any owner who runs on social proof — service businesses, coaches, agencies, SaaS — and
finds that collecting testimonials is a constant, low-yield chore.

## How it works

1. You create a request (which offer or service, plus any context) and send the customer a
   link.
2. They answer about six guided multiple-choice questions in roughly a minute.
3. The model turns their selections into 4–5 testimonial drafts, each a different length and
   emphasis.
4. They get a main draft plus a set of **remix variations** to swap in, can edit the text
   directly, and regenerate for more.
5. They add their name and company, confirm consent, then send it to you or save it. The
   finished testimonial lands in your collection, ready to paste onto a page or into an ad.

## Why it is worth building

- It removes the single biggest reason testimonials don't happen — the blank page.
  Tap-through instead of write-from-scratch turns a ~10% response rate into something much
  higher, and the drafts are usable, not generic.
- Pairs directly with the **Retention & Referral Opportunity Scanner** (No. 12): that flags
  the happy clients worth asking; this makes the ask trivial.
- The collected, cleared-for-use testimonials become a clean input for a sales-page or ads
  workflow.

## Build phases

### Phase 1 — Single-request flow
Editable question set (sensible defaults provided). Send one link; the customer taps
through, gets 4–5 AI drafts, picks / edits / regenerates, and submits with name + consent.
Results stored.

### Phase 2 — Collection and reuse
A "saved testimonials" dashboard — filter by offer, see the implied sentiment from the
answers, copy or export, tag which are cleared for public use, plain-text or embeddable
snippet export.

### Phase 3 — Scale
Bulk-send to a list with reminders, auto-request triggered after a milestone or straight
from the Retention & Referral Scanner, and formatting variants (short pull-quote /
long-form / one-line).

## Stack and dependencies

- A per-request public form (no login for the customer) + your side to build and send requests
- Anthropic API for the drafts, with a fixed variation structure so the options differ
  meaningfully (length, angle, tone)
- Storage for requests, submissions, and consent; name/title capture; export / embed
- Optional trigger integration with the Retention & Referral Opportunity Scanner
- Could live inside Impact Intelligence or ship standalone

## Open questions and risks

- **Authenticity.** An AI-polished testimonial still has to be the customer's real opinion
  and their explicit approval of the final wording — capture affirmative consent and let
  them edit freely, or it's fabrication.
- **Disclosure / FTC.** Testimonials must be genuine and approved by the customer; never
  auto-publish without their sign-off.
- **Sameness.** Five drafts that all sound alike defeats the purpose — the prompt needs
  real variation driven by the answer set.
- **Multiple-choice ceiling.** Pure tap-through can feel generic; one optional "anything in
  your own words?" field lifts quality for little added friction.
- **Gaming.** Nothing stops a business generating fake testimonials — tie each one to a
  real request and recipient so it's at least not frictionless to publish unverified ones.
