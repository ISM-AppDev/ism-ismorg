## The problem

Owners pour effort into new acquisition and neglect the cheaper growth already in the
building — clients who would renew, expand, or refer if asked at the right moment. Churn
signals go unread until the cancellation email arrives, and the happiest client of the year
is never asked for an introduction.

## What it does

Continuously reviews the client journey and customer base for two things:

- **Retention risk** — accounts that have gone quiet, dropped usage, have a contract ending,
  or an unresolved complaint, with the likely reason and a play to address it.
- **Referral and expansion opportunity** — clients hitting a milestone, getting a great
  result, long-tenured, or never asked for a referral, with the ask and the timing.

For each it gives a prioritized action and a drafted message or campaign, and it surfaces
referral-campaign patterns that work in the owner's industry. It re-runs on a schedule so
the list stays current as the base changes.

## Who it is for

Owners with an existing book of clients — retainer, subscription, or repeat-project — who
know retention and referrals are cheaper than acquisition but never systematize them.

## How it works

1. A tool inside the Impact Intelligence platform, using the Business Intelligence Profile
   plus a picture of the client base (stage, tenure, last contact, sentiment) — entered by
   the owner or connected.
2. Each cycle it scans for risk signals and opportunity signals across the base.
3. It produces a ranked list: the client, the signal, why it matters now, the recommended
   play, and a drafted outreach message or a small campaign.
4. It pulls referral-campaign examples that fit the owner's industry and model.
5. Decisions and outcomes are stored, so the scan learns which plays land.

## Why it is worth building

- Retention and referral growth is the highest-margin growth there is, and almost nobody
  runs it as a system. A recurring scan makes it a habit.
- It compounds: churn risks and referral wins feed the Action Advisor; drafted outreach
  drops into the publishing/automation layer.
- The recurring, base-aware nature is the retention hook for the product itself — the list
  is only useful if it keeps running.
- Tiering: base = manual client list + recurring scan + drafted plays; upgrade = connected
  signals (usage, billing, meetings), churn scoring, and triggered automation.

## Build phases

### Phase 1 — Manual base + recurring scan
Owner enters the client list with a few fields. A recurring scan against a fixed set of
risk and opportunity signals; prioritized actions with drafted outreach; industry
referral-campaign examples.

### Phase 2 — Connected signals
Light integrations (CRM, billing/Stripe, calendar) for real signals — usage trends,
payment status, meeting cadence. A churn-risk score with a reason. A referral-campaign
builder.

### Phase 3 — Triggered plays
Fire the play automatically on a signal (send the email, create the task, trigger a gift).
Track retention and referral outcomes. Feed wins back into a pattern library the scan
learns from.

## Stack and dependencies

- Impact Intelligence shell, auth, entitlement gate, Business Intelligence Profile
- A client-base store — manual entry first, then integrations (CRM / Stripe / calendar)
- A signal-scoring model for churn risk and opportunity
- Anthropic API for plays, drafted messages, and industry-pattern research
- `clients`, `retention_signals`, `referral_campaigns` tables
- Scheduler / job runner (shared); publishing/automation layer for Phase 3

## Open questions and risks

- **Data quality.** Without integrations the scan is only as good as what the owner enters,
  and owners won't maintain a big manual list — keep Phase 1 fields minimal.
- **Churn scoring needs history.** A score with no track record isn't credible; early
  versions should present signals, not a confident probability.
- **"Campaigns from your industry"** has to be specific and real, not a generic "ask for
  referrals" — this is where the tool earns trust or loses it.
- **Client-data privacy.** Names and account details are sensitive; be careful what goes
  into research prompts.
