## The problem

Pricing is guesswork wrapped in anxiety. Owners anchor to the number they charged three
years ago, knock 20% off the moment a prospect pauses, and have no read on where the market
actually sits or what their results justify. The fear of quoting too high keeps them
quoting too low.

## What it does

Reviews the way the owner prices, packages, and discounts their offers, and helps optimize
it:

- Market and competitor benchmarks for comparable offers
- Value-based adjustment suggestions tied to the outcome the offer delivers
- Recommended "test" price points, each with a rationale
- A flag on reflexive discounting patterns

The goal is a number the owner can say out loud without flinching, and a way to test it
rather than bet the business on it.

## Who it is for

Service providers, consultants, coaches, and productized-service owners setting a price for
a new offer or working up the nerve to raise an existing one.

## How it works

1. A tool inside the Impact Intelligence platform, using the Business Intelligence Profile
   (offers, deliverables, audience, positioning, results/proof).
2. The owner picks an offer to review.
3. The tool gathers comparable market and competitor pricing (current research), maps the
   offer's value drivers, and proposes two or three options — hold, raise, restructure —
   each with reasoning, the buyer objection it invites, and a way to test it.
4. It flags discounting habits it can see in the offer history.
5. The review is stored so a later price change can be compared against its actual result.

## Why it is worth building

- Pricing is the single highest-leverage number in the business and the one owners are most
  afraid to touch. A tool that gives cover to raise it earns its keep immediately.
- It complements the Offer Strength Analyzer (that judges whether the offer is strong; this
  judges whether it's priced right) and feeds pricing context to What Should I Promote.
- Benchmarks + a test framework is what turns "I think I'm too cheap" into an action.
- Tiering: base = profile-based review with known benchmarks; upgrade = live competitor
  research, package restructuring, and test tracking.

## Build phases

### Phase 1 — Single-offer review
Review one offer against the profile and a manually curated benchmark set. Value-based
recommendation, two or three test price points, objection handling. No live research yet.

### Phase 2 — Live benchmarks and packaging
Current market/competitor research with cited sources. Package restructuring options (tiers,
scope changes, payment terms). Portfolio view — is this offer priced sensibly relative to
the owner's others.

### Phase 3 — Test tracking
Record a price change and its outcome (close rate, revenue, objections heard). Learn the
owner's real floor. Feed pricing context into the Offer Strength Analyzer and Action
Advisor.

## Stack and dependencies

- Impact Intelligence shell, auth, entitlement gate, Business Intelligence Profile
- Shared research layer with source tracking (also used by other Intelligence tools)
- Anthropic API for the analysis and the options; structured output
- `pricing_reviews`, `price_tests` tables

## Open questions and risks

- **Advice boundary.** This must stay "here's the case and how to test it," never "charge
  $X and you'll be fine." Keep it out of prescriptive financial advice.
- **Benchmark comparability.** Two offers with the same name can be wildly different in
  scope; the research has to normalize for that or it misleads.
- **Missing cost data.** True value-based pricing needs the owner's margins and delivery
  cost, which many don't track — the tool has to work without them and say what it's
  assuming.
- **Tone.** The user is anxious. A blunt "you're 40% underpriced" without a path forward
  makes it worse.
