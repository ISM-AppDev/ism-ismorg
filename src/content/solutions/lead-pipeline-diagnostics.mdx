## The problem

Most owners find out their pipeline was thin only when a slow month lands with no warning.
They are "doing marketing," but they have no read on whether it produces enough sales-ready
leads for their revenue goal, where in the process prospects go cold, or which follow-up
they keep skipping. Leads leak out of the gaps between activities and nobody notices.

## What it does

Analyzes the owner's current lead-generation and capture activity, maps it to a simple
pipeline, and produces a diagnostic:

- Do you have enough sales-ready leads for your goal — and if not, by how much?
- Which stage has the worst drop-off, and what is likely causing it?
- Where is follow-up missing or too slow?

Then it recommends specific campaigns and actions to raise lead flow, and provides ready
follow-up and nurture sequences so no lead is left behind. Diagnostic first; templates
second.

## Who it is for

Solo owners and small teams running their own lead generation with no RevOps function,
who need a health check they can act on rather than a dashboard of vanity metrics.

## How it works

1. A tool inside the Impact Intelligence platform, using the shared Business Intelligence
   Profile (offers, audience, revenue goal, current marketing activities).
2. The owner describes — or connects — their lead sources and capture points: forms,
   discovery calls, DMs, events, referrals.
3. The tool estimates lead volume against the revenue goal (with the owner's close rate and
   deal size), finds the stages converting worst, and flags where follow-up is absent or
   delayed.
4. It returns a diagnostic, a prioritized set of fixes ("your webinar registrants get one
   email and then nothing"), and drafted follow-up sequences for the biggest gaps.
5. Stored as a pipeline snapshot so health can be tracked over time and re-checked after
   changes.

## Why it is worth building

- It answers the question owners are most anxious about — "is my pipeline OK?" — with a
  number and a reason, not a feeling.
- The follow-up templates turn a diagnosis into recovered revenue the same week, which
  makes the value obvious.
- It is a natural hub: its gap findings can feed the Action Advisor, and its follow-up
  sequences can drop into Scheduled Posts / email automation.
- Clear tiering: base = self-reported diagnostic + templates; upgrade = connected data,
  drop-off analysis, and low-pipeline alerts.

## Build phases

### Phase 1 — Self-reported diagnostic
Structured intake on sources, rough volumes, close rate, deal size, and current follow-up.
Compute pipeline math against the goal; return gaps, prioritized fixes, and drafted
follow-up sequences. Store the snapshot.

### Phase 2 — Connected data
Light integrations (calendar, a CRM, form tools) to replace estimates with actuals.
Stage-by-stage drop-off analysis. Alert when projected pipeline falls below the goal.

### Phase 3 — Ongoing optimizer
A recurring monitor that nudges on specific misses ("3 leads from last week's event never
got a second touch"), recommends campaigns tuned to what has worked, and hands sequences to
the publishing/automation layer.

## Stack and dependencies

- Impact Intelligence shell, auth, entitlement gate, Business Intelligence Profile
- Structured intake; optional integrations (Google Calendar, a CRM, form platforms)
- A pipeline-math model (volume × conversion × deal size vs goal)
- Anthropic API for the diagnostic narrative, campaign recommendations, and sequence drafts
- `pipeline_snapshots`, `followup_gaps`, `recommendations` tables

## Open questions and risks

- **Self-reported numbers.** Phase 1 is only as good as the owner's inputs; the intake has
  to guide honest estimates and flag low confidence.
- **Integration breadth.** Every CRM and form tool is its own connector — pick two, resist
  the rest until later.
- **"Enough leads" is a function of close rate and deal size**, which many owners don't
  actually know. The tool has to help them estimate those first.
- **Generic advice risk.** Recommendations must name the specific activity and gap, not
  "improve your follow-up."
