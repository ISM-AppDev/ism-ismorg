## The problem

Owners can't tell which part of their revenue system is the actual bottleneck — is it the
offer, the traffic, the follow-up, the automation, or the reporting? So effort gets spread
thin across all five, or spent on the wrong fix. The question they actually ask out loud is
"I'm not sure what we should fix first," and nothing they have gives them a clear answer.

## What it does

A short, self-scored assessment — 20–25 questions, four or five per pillar — across the
five pillars of a revenue system:

- **Offer / pricing** — clarity, conversion, pricing confidence
- **Traffic** — lead volume consistency and source reliability
- **Follow-up** — speed, consistency, and follow-through with leads
- **Automation** — manual bottlenecks and dropped handoffs
- **Reporting** — visibility into what's actually working

Weighted scoring reveals the **single weakest pillar**, and the owner walks away with a
one-page scorecard: their primary leak, why it's limiting growth, and — in general terms —
what fixing it typically involves. The framing throughout is systems-vs-tactics: *what to
fix first* matters more than *what tactic to try next*.

## Who it is for

Established business owners with existing revenue and customer data. Only someone running a
real operating business can answer the questions meaningfully — which is the point: the
assessment qualifies as it diagnoses, and the result segments each owner by which pillar is
weak.

## How it works

1. **Intro framing** sets the tone: why "what to fix first" beats "what tactic next."
2. **Five scored sections**, four or five plain-language questions each, one per pillar. No
   marketing jargon — questions a business owner can answer from what they already know.
3. **Weighted scoring** combines the section scores and surfaces the one pillar most
   limiting growth right now (kept simple, no fabricated benchmarks).
4. **Results page / one-page PDF**: the primary leak, why it matters, and what fixing it
   typically requires — described generally, not prescriptively.
5. **Close**: an invitation to review the scorecard live and decide on one project to
   tackle first.

## Why it is worth building

- It echoes the owner's own words — "I'm not sure what we should fix first" — and runs on
  the business's core diagnostic methodology (five pillars, one weakest link).
- It qualifies while it diagnoses: only owners with an operating business and real data can
  complete it, and the output naturally segments prospects by weak pillar.
- It bridges cleanly to **Your Next Marketing Win Sprint**. The scorecard's whole job is to
  reveal ONE weak pillar — which is exactly the "one defined project" the Sprint is built
  around, so the next step feels obvious rather than sold.
- Landing-page angle: lead with the owner's uncertainty as the headline tension; position
  it as a diagnostic that reveals one clear answer, not a quiz that lists everything wrong.
  Consultative, systems-minded, no ROI promises.

## Build phases

### Phase 1 — Validate the scoring
Write the five-pillar question set (4–5 each) and the weighting logic in a spreadsheet.
Run it against a handful of real or composite business profiles to make sure results feel
specific, not generic. Draft the one-page PDF template by hand.

### Phase 2 — Ship the assessment
Turn the validated logic into an interactive form (a lightweight quiz tool, or Typeform /
Google Form + a Zapier-to-PDF step, or a small custom form). Auto-generate the one-page
scorecard: primary leak, why it matters, what fixing it typically involves. Capture the
opt-in.

### Phase 3 — Nurture and bridge
A four-email follow-up after opt-in:
1. Deliver results + reinforce what the weakest-pillar score means in practice
2. Why fixing the weakest pillar first beats spreading effort across all five
3. A short composite/anonymized walkthrough of identifying one pillar leading to a focused fix
4. A direct invite to book the results call, framed as "deciding which project to run in the Sprint"

Plus a booking link for the results call and segment tags by weakest pillar feeding the
Sprint's "one project" framing.

## Stack and dependencies

- Scoring logic validated in a spreadsheet before any build
- A form / quiz tool (or a small custom form) + PDF generation (Zapier-to-PDF or similar)
- Email platform for the four-email sequence; a booking link for the results call
- Segment tags on opt-in by weakest pillar
- **Depends on tightening the Sprint offer messaging first** (known 54/100 weakness) so the
  post-scorecard call has a compelling next step to point to
- Bridges to: **Your Next Marketing Win Sprint**

## Open questions and risks

- **Unvalidated-feeling scoring** would undercut the "judgment, not tactics" positioning —
  the weighting has to be tested against real profiles before promotion.
- **No implied ROI guarantees.** Results describe what fixing a pillar typically involves,
  in general terms, never a promised outcome.
- **Sprint offer weakness (54/100)** may blunt conversion right after a strong diagnostic —
  worth tightening Sprint messaging before promoting the scorecard heavily.
- **Qualification cuts both ways** — requiring real business data is great for lead quality
  but limits top-of-funnel volume; that's an accepted trade, not a bug.
