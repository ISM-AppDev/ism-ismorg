## The problem

"I don't have time to create content."

You already did the hard part. You ran the webinar, gave the talk, or hosted the training,
and an hour of your best thinking is sitting in a recording. Then it stays there. Turning it
into posts, an email, and an article means re-watching it, picking out the good parts,
writing in your own voice, and doing it again for every channel. Most people never start.

## What it does

Upload a Zoom recording, or just its transcript, and get back a full set of content from
that one session:

- **10 to 15 social posts**, each built around a different moment, in your voice
- **A recap email** for the people who attended and the people who didn't
- **A blog draft** that reads like an article, not a transcript

Everything is drawn from what was actually said, with real quotes attributed to the right
speaker, and nothing invented to fill space.

## Who it is for

Anyone who talks for a living and records it: coaches, consultants, speakers, event hosts,
and teams that run regular webinars or trainings.

## How it works

1. **Upload the recording or transcript** straight from Zoom. No cleanup needed first.
2. **It reads the whole session** and finds the strongest moments: the stories, the
   frameworks, the memorable lines.
3. **It drafts the set** using your stored voice, offers, and audience, so each piece sounds
   like you and ends with the right next step.
4. **You review and adjust**, then copy the pieces out or send them to scheduling.

## Why it is worth building

The honest challenge: anyone can paste a transcript into their own Claude account and ask
for posts. The product only wins if it does what a chat window can't.

1. **It takes the real input.** A recording can't be pasted into a chat. Raw Zoom
   transcripts are also messy and long, and quality drops on a 90-minute session. The
   product accepts the file as it comes and handles the rest.
2. **It remembers who you are.** Voice, offers, audience, and calls to action are stored once
   instead of re-explained in every prompt. This is the Business Intelligence Profile idea
   from Impact Intelligence.
3. **Every quote is real and traceable.** A chat tends to paraphrase and invent quotes. This
   pulls verbatim lines with the speaker and timestamp, and flags anything that needs a
   guest's permission.
4. **It finds the best moments, not just a summary.** It picks the strongest moments and
   gives the timestamps for cutting clips, which a chat can't do for video.
5. **It finishes the job.** The posts are spread across a calendar and handed to scheduling
   (see **Scheduled Posts**, No. 7), instead of leaving you a block of text to copy out.
6. **One event, two outputs.** Pairs with **Chat Navigator** (No. 18). Chat Navigator reads
   the event's chat and tells you who to follow up with. This reads the recording and tells
   you what to publish. The chat also shows what the audience asked, which can guide which
   moments become posts. Sold together it is a post-event kit: content for the audience and a
   follow-up list for the people who attended.

## Positioning

Don't pitch it as "AI writes your posts." Pitch it as **one event in, a full post-event kit
out.** The first is a commodity. The second is a workflow a chat window can't give you.

## Build phases

### Phase 1: Transcript to kit
Upload a transcript. Get the social posts, recap email, and blog draft, matched to a stored
voice and set of offers. This is the part that can also be done in a chat, so it needs the
stored profile to earn its place.

### Phase 2: Recording and moments
Accept the Zoom recording, transcribe it, find the strongest moments, and attach verbatim
quotes with speaker and timestamp.

### Phase 3: Clips and scheduling
Cut captioned clips from the best moments, and place the posts on a calendar for scheduling.
Optionally take the event's chat file from Chat Navigator to shape the content around real
audience questions.

## Stack and dependencies

- File upload for Zoom recordings and transcripts, with transcription for recordings
- Anthropic API for moment-finding and drafting, with a stored profile for voice and offers
- Video tooling for clips (Phase 3)
- Optional hand-off to Scheduled Posts and to Chat Navigator

## Open questions and risks

- **Transcription cost and length.** Long recordings are expensive to transcribe and read.
  Needs a credit or usage model and a sensible length limit.
- **Guest and attendee permission.** Quoting a guest speaker or an attendee may need their
  consent. The product should flag it rather than assume.
- **Sameness.** Fifteen posts that all sound alike defeats the purpose. The drafts need real
  variation in angle and format.
- **Phase 1 is easy to copy.** The durable value is the profile, the timestamps, the clips,
  and the scheduling, not the first draft.
