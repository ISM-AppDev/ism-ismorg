## The problem

Even good tools only help when the owner remembers to open them. Between busy weeks, the
Impact Intelligence tool set sits unused and the subscription starts to feel optional. And
the intelligence that would actually move the needle — what changed for *this* business's
audience and offers — never gets assembled unless someone sits down to assemble it.

## What it does

The Impact Intelligence Brief is a recurring email (weekly, biweekly, or monthly,
owner's choice) that combines the Business Intelligence Profile, prior Impact Intelligence findings,
the latest Audience Pulse research, and fresh external validation for that cycle into one
short executive-style brief. It answers a single question: **what changed or matters for my
business, audience, offers, and marketing decisions right now?**

Recommended structure:

1. **What changed** — important new developments since the last brief
2. **What your audience is saying** — current concerns, objections, questions, language
3. **Competitor & market signals** — relevant moves among chosen competitors or the category
4. **Potential growth opportunities** — messaging, offer, or lead-gen openings worth testing
5. **What I recommend you focus on** — a single prioritized recommendation
6. **Your next best action** — one concrete step, linked straight into the relevant tool

It is not a newsletter and not a summary of public news. Sourced facts are marked separately
from AI interpretation, with timestamps and references.

## Who it is for

Owners on the higher Impact Intelligence tiers. This feature is the reason Impact Intelligence is a subscription rather than
a pack of tools — it's the proactive layer.

## How it works

1. A **higher-tier** capability in Impact Intelligence. Not available on the entry plan.
2. The owner sets a delivery frequency; the architecture supports adding frequencies later
   without schema changes.
3. On each cycle a scheduled job assembles inputs: shared profile, recent tool runs,
   Audience Pulse findings, and a fresh research pass. The model drafts the six-section brief.
4. Every brief and its supporting findings are stored, so future briefs avoid repetition,
   show change over time, and can reference past recommendations.
5. Email delivery is a first-class concern: store scheduled/generated/sent dates, failure
   state, retries. Owners can pause, change frequency, and resume without losing history.
6. Each "next best action" deep-links into Message Market-Fit Checker, Offer Strength
   Analyzer, Lead Magnet Opportunity Finder, or What Should I Promote?.

## Why it is worth building

- It is the front door of the higher tiers. The brief identifies what deserves
  attention; the existing tools help the owner act on it.
- Proactive delivery is the strongest retention mechanism in the suite — value arrives
  whether or not the owner logs in.
- The stored history creates a switching cost: leaving means losing the running record of
  what changed and what was tried.

## Build phases

### Phase 1 — Manual brief, on-screen
Assemble profile + recent runs + one research pass into the six-section format. Render in
the app. Store the brief. No email, no schedule.

### Phase 2 — Scheduled email delivery
Frequency setting, scheduled job, email send with full delivery-status tracking, pause /
resume / change frequency. Historical archive in the app.

### Phase 3 — Cumulative and linked
Diff against prior briefs ("new / still important / fading"). Deep links from "next best
action" into each tool. Off-cycle alerts for high-threshold events. Competitor-monitoring
and emerging-buyer-language sections powered by Audience Pulse sub-modules.

## Stack and dependencies

- Impact Intelligence shell, auth, **higher-tier entitlement gate**, Business Intelligence Profile
- Depends on **Audience Pulse** (research engine) and benefits from **What Should I Promote?**
- Shared research/validation layer with source + freshness tracking; fact vs inference separation
- Email delivery service (Resend) with per-message status records
- Scheduler / job runner; `intelligence_briefs`, `email_delivery_history`, `delivery_preferences`, `scheduled_report_jobs` tables
- Anthropic API for drafting; duplicate-suppression against `previously_reported_findings`

## Open questions and risks

- **Repetition.** Without solid diffing against past briefs, month three reads like month
  one and the owner unsubscribes. The comparison mechanism is make-or-break.
- **Research cost and reliability.** Per-user external research every cycle — needs
  caching, source allow-listing, and a fallback when research is thin.
- **Deliverability.** Recurring AI-generated email has spam-filter risk; needs proper
  domain auth and send reputation management.
- **Generic drift.** The brief must stay specific to the business. A generic-sounding brief
  is worse than no brief.
- **Frequency default.** Monthly is the safe default for signal-to-noise; let owners opt
  into more.
