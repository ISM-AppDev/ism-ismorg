## The problem

Consistency beats brilliance on social, and almost nobody is consistent. Owners know they
should post a few times a week, start strong, and fall off inside a month because every
post is a blank-page decision made at the wrong time of day. Generic AI post generators
don't fix it — they produce off-voice filler that the owner won't publish, so the habit
still dies.

## What it does

A standing queue of posts, drafted ahead of time in the owner's voice, on a cadence they
set. Each draft is built from the shared business profile — their positioning, offers,
audience, past posts, current priorities — so it sounds like them and points somewhere
useful. The owner reviews the queue and edits or regenerates anything weak.

Approving a post isn't the end of it: the owner then **schedules it** — onto the next open
cadence slot or a specific date and time they pick — and a **calendar** shows the whole
month at a glance: what's already scheduled, what's published, and which slots are still
open. From there each post either publishes automatically or comes back as a one-click
"approve and post" at its slot time.

The unit of value is the **filled calendar**: at any moment there are two or three weeks of
scheduled posts, so "what do I post today" is never a question again.

## Who it is for

Solo owners, consultants, and small teams who want to show up regularly on LinkedIn (and
later other channels) without it becoming a daily writing job. Also a fit for someone
rebuilding a dormant profile who needs momentum without a content sprint.

## How it works

1. Ships as a tool in SOTS Connect, using shared auth and the shared business profile. On
   first run it ingests the owner's recent posts to learn cadence, structure, and voice.
2. The owner sets a schedule (e.g. Tue/Thu 8am) and a rough mix — how much teaching vs story vs
   offer vs opinion.
3. A generation pass fills the queue two to three weeks out. Each draft carries its source
   angle ("this one leans on your case-study library") and a confidence note.
4. The owner works the queue: approve, edit inline, regenerate with a nudge. Each approved
   post is placed on the schedule — the next open cadence slot, or a custom date and time —
   and appears on a calendar next to everything already scheduled and published. Slots can
   be dragged to reschedule.
5. At its slot the post publishes automatically (Phase 3) or the owner gets a one-click
   "approve and post" email. Published posts feed back so the voice model keeps tightening.

Inspired by the "Scheduled Posts" pattern in the evyus workflow catalog — the retention
idea being that something useful shows up on a schedule and asks you to finish it before it
sends more.

## Why it is worth building

- It is the backbone content habit. Almost every other Brand & Visibility idea here
  (Daily Newsjack, repurposing, FAQ-to-post) can drop its output *into this queue* rather
  than reinventing review-and-publish.
- The filled-queue mechanic is sticky: the owner accumulates weeks of scheduled value they
  would lose by leaving.
- Voice fidelity from the profile + past-post ingestion is the moat. A cold generic tool
  can't match it, and the owner feels the difference immediately.
- Natural upsell path: base = draft queue you copy out; upgrade = auto-publish + analytics.

## Build phases

### Phase 1 — Draft queue + calendar
Pull voice + positioning + offers from the profile, ingest recent posts, generate a rolling
queue of drafts with a simple mix control. Owner approves / edits / regenerates, then
assigns each approved post a date and time and sees a calendar of what's scheduled /
published / open. Posting is still manual at this stage (copy out at the slot, or a
reminder fires). Store drafts, schedule, and decisions.

### Phase 2 — Cadence automation and feedback
Recurring cadence slots that auto-fill from the queue, auto-refill when queue depth runs
low, drag-to-reschedule on the calendar, "more like this / less like this" feedback. Accept
drafts pushed in from other tools (Daily Newsjack, repurposing, FAQ-to-post).

### Phase 3 — Publish and learn
Direct scheduled publishing to LinkedIn (+ approve-by-email). Pull post performance back in;
tune the voice model and the content mix from what actually landed. Pause/resume without
losing the queue.

## Stack and dependencies

- SOTS Connect shell, auth, entitlement gate, shared profile (voice, positioning, offers, priorities)
- Past-post ingestion + a stored voice profile (shared with other content tools)
- Anthropic API for drafting; structured drafts with angle + confidence metadata
- `scheduled_posts` table: draft, status (drafted / approved / scheduled / published / skipped), scheduled_at, cadence_slot_id, source angle, performance — the calendar view reads from this
- `cadence_slots` table: recurring day/time rules the queue auto-fills (Phase 2)
- Scheduler / job runner (shared); LinkedIn API + OAuth (Phase 3); email approve-by-reply (Resend)

## Open questions and risks

- **Voice drift.** Regeneration and edits have to feed back or the queue slowly stops
  sounding like the owner.
- **Auto-publish trust.** Most owners will want approve-first for a long time; auto-publish
  is opt-in per slot, not a global switch.
- **Queue staleness.** A draft written three weeks ago can be overtaken by events — need a
  "still fresh?" check before it publishes.
- **Scheduling correctness.** Owner timezone, DST, "best time to post" defaults, and two
  posts landing in the same slot are all small things that erode trust in the calendar if
  they're wrong.
- **Channel scope.** LinkedIn first. X, Facebook, Threads, Bluesky each add API and format
  work; don't promise them in Phase 1.
- **Overlap with Daily Newsjack.** Keep this the *queue and publishing* layer; Newsjack is
  one of several *sources* that feed it.
